Moving in with friends is great — until the first bill arrives and everyone has a slightly different idea of what "fair" means.

Here's a practical system for splitting household expenses that covers rent, utilities, and the inevitable edge cases like "but I was away for two weeks" and "I don't use Netflix".

## The Two Approaches to House Splitting

**Equal split:** Every housemate pays the same amount for everything. Simple to calculate, easy to automate, and removes the need for ongoing negotiation.

**Proportional or custom split:** Each person pays based on room size, income, or usage. More equitable in some situations but requires agreement upfront and more ongoing calculation.

Most houses use equal split for most things and make exceptions for specific situations (like someone having a larger room paying more rent).

## Rent

Rent is usually the biggest and most divisive expense.

**Equal split:** Divide the total rent by the number of people. Works well if rooms are similar in size.

**Room-based split:** Bigger room = higher share. A simple way to do this: total up the square meterage of all bedrooms, calculate each person's percentage, apply that to the total rent. EasySplit supports custom split percentages for exactly this situation.

**Fixed amounts:** Some housemates negotiate fixed amounts for each room regardless of the calculation above. This often reflects who has the ensuite, who has the balcony, and so on.

Set up rent as a recurring expense in EasySplit. Enter the due date, the amount, and how it's split — the app will notify everyone when it's due and log it automatically each month.

## Utilities

Electricity, gas, water, internet — these are usually split equally unless there's a clear reason not to.

**The "I was away" conversation:** Someone goes on holiday for three weeks and thinks they shouldn't pay full share of electricity. In practice, unless they've also turned off the hot water system and the fridge, the baseline usage doesn't drop much. Most houses just split utilities equally regardless of time away.

If you want to accommodate this, a simple approach: have the person who was away pay a reduced share (e.g., 50%) for the time they were genuinely absent. Log the adjustment in EasySplit.

## Groceries

This is where equal splitting breaks down most often. One person eats at home every day; another eats out constantly. One person is vegan; another buys expensive cuts of meat.

Options:
1. **No splitting at all:** Everyone buys their own food. Common in sharehouse situations with people who have very different lifestyles.
2. **Split communal groceries, not personal ones:** Keep a shared basket for things everyone uses (milk, butter, cooking oil, cleaning products, toilet paper) and split that. Personal food is each person's problem.
3. **Equal split everything:** Works for households that genuinely eat together most nights.

EasySplit lets you split a single expense among only certain housemates — useful when some people opt out of the communal food arrangement.

## Subscriptions

Netflix, Spotify, Amazon Prime, Disney+. These are easy to split — divide the monthly cost by the number of people using it.

Use EasySplit's recurring expense feature: set up each subscription with its renewal date and the people splitting it. The app logs it automatically on renewal.

The "but I don't use it" problem: if someone genuinely doesn't use a streaming service, don't force them to split it. Better a fair arrangement than ongoing resentment.

## One-Off Household Expenses

Cleaning supplies, lightbulbs, kitchen appliances, furniture — these pop up irregularly and are easy to forget.

**The rule:** if you buy something for the house, log it in EasySplit immediately. Don't wait until the end of the month to try to remember everything you've spent on household stuff.

## Setting Up a House Account

Some households use a shared transaction account. Everyone transfers a fixed amount in each month (say, $50) and the housemate who shops for communal stuff pays from that account.

The advantage: no individual reimbursements needed.
The downside: someone has to manage the account, and you need trust that it's being managed fairly.

EasySplit works well alongside this model — you can still log what's being purchased from the shared pool and who contributed, so everyone can see the history.

## When Someone Isn't Paying

This is the hardest conversation in any sharehouse.

First, make sure it's not a cash flow problem — some people get paid at different times of the month. EasySplit's balance view shows exactly what's outstanding, which removes ambiguity from the conversation.

If someone consistently delays payment, address it directly with a private conversation, not a passive-aggressive message in the group chat. The goal is to resolve it, not to publicly shame anyone.
