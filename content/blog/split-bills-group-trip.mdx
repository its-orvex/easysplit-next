Group trips are one of the best things in life. Twelve people on a villa in Bali, splitting the cost of something you couldn't afford alone. But at some point on every group trip, someone has to have the money conversation — and it's awkward.

Here's how to handle it well before you leave, during the trip, and when you're settling up at the end.

## Before You Leave

The most important thing you can do is agree on the system before anyone books anything.

**Decide who pays for what upfront.** Some expenses are naturally collective (the villa, the driver, big group dinners). Others are personal (your flights, your shopping, that extra massage). Draw the line clearly before you go.

**Set up a group in EasySplit.** Create a trip, share the invite code in your WhatsApp group, and have everyone join. Now anyone can log expenses as they happen — no waiting until the end to figure out who owes what.

**Discuss splitting styles.** Equal splits work great if everyone is in the same financial position. If not, talk about it. Some groups do proportional splits based on income. Some people opt out of certain activities. Have this conversation before you're standing at a restaurant deciding who pays.

## During the Trip

### Flights

Flights are almost always split individually — everyone books their own. The exception is if someone is organising a group booking, in which case they often front the money and collect from others.

Log the person's name and amount in EasySplit. Don't wait — memories fade fast.

### Accommodation

The villa or Airbnb is usually the easiest big expense to split equally. Total cost ÷ number of people. Simple.

If some people have nicer rooms (or one couple is sharing a room that counts as two people), adjust accordingly.

### Food and Drink

This is where it gets complicated. Group dinners are easy — whoever pays logs it in EasySplit and splits it equally (or by item if people ordered very different things).

But what about the person who grabbed breakfast for everyone from the warung? The late-night snack run? Log everything, even the small ones. They add up fast on a 10-day trip.

EasySplit's receipt scanning is great here — you take a photo of the receipt and the app pulls out the amount. Log it in 10 seconds.

### Activities

Some people will do every activity. Some won't. Don't force everyone to split the cost of an activity they didn't do.

Log each activity separately in EasySplit with only the people who participated marked for that expense. The app handles the maths.

### The Shared Pool

Some groups create a shared pool — everyone puts in $300 at the start and the group treasurer spends from it on collective expenses. This simplifies day-to-day logistics.

Log the initial contributions in EasySplit as one expense. Then log spending from the pool normally. At the end, you'll see who the net contributors and net receivers were.

## Settling Up at the End

This is where EasySplit's settlement algorithm shines. Instead of everyone transferring to everyone, the app calculates the minimum number of transfers.

Eight people on a trip might have 28 possible transfer directions. EasySplit usually reduces this to 4 or 5 actual transfers using PayID.

**How to settle up:**
1. Open the Balances tab in EasySplit
2. See who owes what to whom
3. Tap Pay Now on each settlement
4. Your banking app opens with the exact amount and the recipient's PayID pre-filled
5. Confirm the transfer in your banking app
6. Mark as settled in EasySplit

The whole process takes about two minutes per person.

## Tips for a Stress-Free Money Split

- Log expenses as they happen, not at the end of the trip
- Take photos of every receipt, even if you don't scan them (good backup)
- Don't let balances sit for months — settle up within a week of getting home
- If someone is consistently reluctant to settle up, address it directly after the first trip, not after the third

Group trips should be memorable for the right reasons. Sorting the money out properly at the start means you spend the week focused on the trip, not the maths.
